## The principle first

Before the exercises: the exercise you choose matters far less than how hard you train it. Any decent chest movement, taken to 1 rep in reserve (1 RIR) with good form and progressed over time, will build muscle. The movements below are the best options available — but two hard working sets of a "lesser" exercise beats five sloppy sets of the "perfect" one every time.

The approach for everything in this guide: **2 working sets, 1 RIR, progressive overload.** That's it. No junk volume.

## Best Chest Exercises

### 1. Smith Machine Incline Bench Press

The best starting point for most people. The fixed bar path removes the stabilisation demand of a barbell or dumbbells, which means you can focus entirely on driving with the chest and push closer to failure safely. The incline angle loads the upper chest — the area most people are weakest and where development makes the biggest visual difference.

The Smith machine gets unfairly dismissed. For hypertrophy, the ability to train safely close to failure without a spotter is a genuine advantage over free weights.

### 2. Dumbbell Incline Bench Press

The free-weight alternative to Smith incline. Dumbbells allow a greater range of motion and a deeper stretch at the bottom, which research consistently links to greater muscle growth. They also force both sides to work independently, exposing and correcting strength imbalances.

Slightly harder to push to failure safely than the Smith machine, but excellent for upper chest development.

### 3. Pec Deck Fly

The best chest isolation movement available. The pec deck keeps constant tension on the pecs through the full range and removes the triceps and shoulders from the equation entirely, so the chest does all the work. The stretched position at full extension is where most of the growth stimulus comes from.

Use this as a second movement after pressing rather than a primary exercise.

### 4. Chest Dips

A brilliant lower and outer chest builder. The key technique point: lean your torso forward to bias the chest rather than the triceps. Keep elbows slightly flared rather than tucked close to the body. Add weight with a dip belt once bodyweight becomes easy — this is a movement you can load heavily over time.

### 5. Cable Crossovers

Cables keep constant tension throughout the range of motion, which machines and free weights can't. Run them **low-to-high** to target the upper chest, or **high-to-low** to target the lower chest. The ability to switch direction makes this one of the most versatile chest movements available.

Excellent as a finisher after pressing — chase the contraction and squeeze hard at the peak.

### 6. Chest Press Machine

Any well-designed chest press machine is a legitimate pressing option. Machines let you push to near-failure without any spotter or balance requirement, the resistance curve is usually better than free weights, and your joints stay in a consistent path which reduces injury risk. Don't dismiss machines — for hypertrophy purposes they're outstanding.

## How to train chest

A complete chest session needs two things: a pressing movement and a stretch-focused fly. You don't need six exercises. Pick one press and one fly, do 2 hard sets of each, and go home. If you want slightly more volume, add a second pressing variation.

A good pairing: **Smith Machine Incline Press + Pec Deck Fly.** Or **Dumbbell Incline Press + Cable Crossovers.** Train each to 1 RIR, add weight when you can, and repeat.
