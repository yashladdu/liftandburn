## The treadmill workout that broke the internet

In 2020, a 24-year-old named Lauren Giraldo posted a TikTok explaining the treadmill routine that helped her lose 30 pounds. The formula was simple: incline 12, speed 3 mph, duration 30 minutes. She called it 12-3-30.

The video went viral. Then it went viral again. Then fitness influencers tested it, doctors commented on it, and gym-goers around the world started queuing for treadmills to try it. As of 2024 the hashtag has accumulated billions of views.

The question worth asking: does it actually work, or is this just another fitness trend that looks good on camera?

The honest answer is that 12-3-30 works — and the reasons it works are grounded in legitimate exercise science, not social media hype. This article breaks down exactly what it is, why it is effective, how many calories it burns, and how to do it correctly.

---

## What is the 12-3-30 workout?

The 12-3-30 workout is a treadmill protocol with three fixed variables:

- **Incline: 12%**
- **Speed: 3 mph (4.8 km/h)**
- **Duration: 30 minutes**

That is the entire protocol. No warm-up intervals, no sprints, no changing the settings mid-session. You set the treadmill to 12% incline and 3 mph, walk for 30 minutes, and you are done.

It sounds almost too simple. That simplicity is part of why it went viral — and part of why it works. The barrier to entry is extremely low. Anyone can walk. Anyone can press two buttons on a treadmill. And at 3 mph, virtually nobody needs to hold the handrails.

## How to do it correctly

The protocol is simple but there are several form points that determine whether you get maximum benefit or undermine the session entirely.

### Step 1 — Set the treadmill correctly

Set the incline to **12%** and the speed to **3.0 mph (4.8 km/h)**. Do not approximate. The specific combination of these two numbers is what puts most people in the right intensity zone — challenging but sustainable for 30 minutes.

If you are converting to km/h: 3 mph = 4.8 km/h. Set it to 4.8.

### Step 2 — Do not hold the handrails

This is the most important rule and the most commonly broken one.

Holding the handrails transfers a significant portion of your bodyweight to your arms, dramatically reducing the calorie burn and neutering the effect of the incline. Research suggests holding the rails can reduce calorie expenditure by 20-25% at steep inclines.

If you cannot walk at 12% incline at 3 mph without holding on, reduce the incline to 8 or 10% until you build the strength and balance to let go. Do not cheat the incline with your hands.

### Step 3 — Walk with intention

Drive through your heels, engage your glutes, and keep your torso upright. At 12% incline your posterior chain — glutes, hamstrings, calves — should be doing most of the work. Leaning forward excessively onto the belt reduces glute engagement and increases lower back strain.

### Step 4 — Maintain the full 30 minutes

The protocol is 30 minutes of continuous walking. If you need to step off for 30 seconds early on, that is fine as you build fitness. The goal is to work up to completing the full 30 minutes without stopping or holding the rails.

### Step 5 — Optional warm-up and cool-down

Lauren Giraldo's original protocol does not include a formal warm-up, but spending 2-3 minutes at a lower incline (3-5%) before ramping to 12% is sensible — particularly if you are training first thing in the morning. A 2-minute cool-down at flat walking pace helps bring your heart rate down gradually.

## How many calories does 12-3-30 burn?

The calorie burn depends primarily on your bodyweight. Heavier individuals burn more calories for any given exercise because they are moving more mass against gravity with every step.

Here are realistic estimates for 30 minutes of 12-3-30 using MET-based calculations:

| Body weight | Estimated calories burned |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| 60 kg (132 lbs) | 280–320 kcal |
| 70 kg (154 lbs) | 320–370 kcal |
| 80 kg (176 lbs) | 370–420 kcal |
| 90 kg (198 lbs) | 415–475 kcal |
| 100 kg (220 lbs) | 460–530 kcal |

For context, a 75kg person burning approximately 350 calories in 30 minutes is roughly equivalent to a moderate-intensity run — but at a walking pace with a fraction of the joint impact.

Over a week of daily 12-3-30 sessions, a 75kg person burns approximately 2,400–2,600 calories from the workout alone. Over a month, that is approaching 10,000 calories — the equivalent of roughly 1.3kg of fat, purely from this one daily habit.

## Why it works so well

The 12-3-30 workout is not magic. It works because it applies several well-established principles of effective fat-loss cardio — it just packages them in a format simple enough that people actually do it consistently.

### 1. Incline multiplies calorie burn without multiplying recovery cost

At 12% incline, your body works significantly harder than flat walking at the same speed. The ACSM metabolic equation for treadmill walking shows that grade has nearly twice the metabolic impact per unit increase compared to speed.

Walking at 4.8 km/h on a 12% incline burns roughly the same calories as jogging at 8–9 km/h on a flat surface — but with near-zero impact force on your joints. This means you can do it daily without the accumulated joint stress that forces runners to take recovery days.

### 2. It lands most people squarely in Zone 2

At 12% incline and 3 mph, most people's heart rate settles between 120–145 bpm — which for the average adult falls in Zone 2, the heart rate range of 60–70% of maximum.

Zone 2 is the optimal zone for fat oxidation. Your body primarily burns fat as fuel, not glycogen. This means 12-3-30 is genuinely burning fat — not just burning calories from whatever you ate that morning.

Zone 2 also produces meaningful cardiovascular adaptations over time: improved mitochondrial density, better fat oxidation efficiency, and a stronger aerobic base. These are not cosmetic changes — they are functional improvements that make all other exercise easier.

### 3. It is sustainable — which is the most underrated quality in any workout

The single biggest predictor of fat loss results is consistency over time. A workout you do every day for three months beats a more intense workout you do twice a week for six weeks and then quit.

12-3-30 succeeds on consistency grounds because:

- **It is low impact** — your joints do not object to daily use
- **It does not require recovery days** — you can walk tomorrow after walking today
- **It is not psychologically brutal** — unlike high-intensity intervals, it does not require you to talk yourself into starting
- **It fits into any schedule** — 30 minutes is achievable before work, during lunch, or after dinner
- **It is the same every time** — no thinking, no programming, just walk

### 4. It works your posterior chain

At 12% incline, every step requires meaningful glute and hamstring engagement to drive your body uphill. Over 30 minutes that is thousands of posterior chain contractions — which over weeks and months produces visible changes in glute development and overall lower body strength.

This is why many people who commit to 12-3-30 report not just fat loss but visible changes to their lower body shape — particularly the glutes — that flat walking at the same speed and duration would never produce.

### 5. The numbers are memorable and repeatable

This sounds trivial but it is not. The reason most people do not stick to gym routines is cognitive load — too many variables, too many decisions, too much to remember. 12-3-30 removes all of that. Twelve. Three. Thirty. You never have to think about what to do. You just do it.

## Who it works best for

**Beginners and returning exercisers.** If you have not trained consistently before or are returning after a long break, 12-3-30 is an ideal entry point. The intensity is appropriate, the learning curve is nonexistent, and the consistency it builds translates to better results over time.

**People who find running unsustainable.** Running is high impact, requires recovery days, and has a meaningful injury rate. 12-3-30 gives you similar calorie burn without any of those costs.

**People who lift weights and need low-interference cardio.** Because 12-3-30 is steady-state Zone 2 work, it does not significantly impair strength training recovery. It can be done on the same day as lifting — or daily — without eating into your gains.

**Anyone who has tried and abandoned other cardio protocols.** The consistency advantage of 12-3-30 is its greatest strength. If you have started and quit HIIT, running programmes, or cycling routines, the low barrier to entry and zero-skill-required nature of 12-3-30 makes it worth trying as a long-term habit.

---

## The one limitation worth knowing

12-3-30 is an excellent fat-loss and cardiovascular tool. It is not a strength training programme. If your goal includes building muscle, you need to be lifting weights alongside it — 12-3-30 alone will not build meaningful muscle mass.

The ideal approach for most people: lift weights 3-4 days per week, do 12-3-30 on the remaining days (or daily if recovery allows). This combination — heavy lifting plus consistent Zone 2 cardio — is exactly the hybrid athlete model that produces the most complete physical results.

---

## Should you try it?

Yes — with realistic expectations.

12-3-30 will not transform your body in two weeks. But done consistently — five to seven days per week for two to three months — it will produce meaningful fat loss, improved cardiovascular fitness, stronger glutes and hamstrings, and the kind of daily movement habit that compounds over time.

The viral attention it received is deserved, not because it is revolutionary, but because it is a legitimate, well-designed protocol that happens to be simple enough for almost anyone to do consistently.

Set the incline to 12. Set the speed to 3. Walk for 30 minutes. Let go of the rails. Do it again tomorrow.
