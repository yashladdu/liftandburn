## What legs actually need

Legs are the most undertrained major muscle group for most people, and also the one people most often overtrain when they do focus on them. You don't need five quad exercises in a session. You need a quad movement, a hamstring movement, and some calf work — done hard with progressive overload.

The categories: **quads**, **hamstrings + glutes**, **calves**. Cover all three and you've trained legs properly. **2 working sets per exercise, 1 RIR.**

---

## Best Quad Exercises

### 1. Hack Squat

One of the best quad builders available. The fixed pad position and plate-loaded path let you push heavy and close to failure with less technical demand than a barbell squat. Foot placement higher on the platform = more glute involvement; lower = more quad. For quad emphasis, keep feet relatively low and let the knees travel forward.

### 2. Smith Machine Squat

An excellent alternative to the free-bar squat, especially for people without a spotter or who struggle with barbell position. The guided path lets you focus on driving with the quads rather than on balance and technique. Place feet slightly forward from directly under the bar to allow natural squat mechanics.

### 3. Leg Press

Lets you train the quads with serious load and no balance or stability requirement whatsoever. Easy to push to near-failure safely. Foot placement matters: shoulder-width, toes slightly out, feet roughly mid-platform gives the most balanced quad + glute stimulus. Partial range of motion is common — go as deep as your hips allow without your lower back rounding.

### 4. Leg Extension

The best isolation exercise for the quads, particularly the sweep of the outer quad and the teardrop near the knee. Constant tension, easy to push to failure, no hip involvement. Perfect as a finisher after compound quad work. Pause or squeeze briefly at the top for an added stimulus.

### 5. Barbell Squat

The classic. Trains quads, glutes, hamstrings, and forces the whole body to brace and stabilise under load. A high-bar squat (bar across the upper traps) tends to be more quad-dominant; a low-bar squat shifts some load to the hamstrings and back. Technically demanding to do well, but highly rewarding when you do.

### 6. Bulgarian Split Squat

Humbling and highly effective. One foot elevated behind you on a bench, drop the rear knee toward the floor. The unilateral loading exposes side-to-side imbalances and builds serious quad and glute strength. One of the most effective leg movements there is — most people avoid it for the wrong reasons (it's hard).

## Best Hamstring and Glute Exercises

### 7. Romanian Deadlift

The best movement for the hamstring-glute tie-in. Keep a soft knee bend, hinge at the hips, and lower the bar with control until you feel a deep stretch in the hamstrings, then drive the hips forward to return. The stretch position at the bottom is where most of the growth signal comes from. A staple for anyone who wants complete leg development.

### 8. Seated Leg Curl

Slightly better than the lying variation for hamstring growth because of the stretch position it places the hamstrings in. Research consistently shows that training muscles in a lengthened position produces more hypertrophy. The primary direct hamstring exercise in most programmes. Full range of motion — let the weight stretch the hamstring completely before curling back up.

### 9. Lying Leg Curl

Works equally well if seated isn't available. The principle is the same — full range of motion, controlled eccentric, squeeze at the top. A solid hamstring movement by any standard.

## Best Calf Exercises

### 10. Standing Calf Raise

Targets the gastrocnemius — the larger, outer calf muscle that's visible from behind. The key technique point most people miss: **full range of motion with a proper stretch at the bottom**. Bouncing through short-range partials is nearly useless for calf growth. Lower the heel as far down as the platform allows, pause briefly to eliminate the stretch reflex, then raise fully and squeeze.

Calves are notoriously stubborn to grow and respond better to higher reps (12-20 range) than most muscle groups.

### 11. Seated Calf Raise / Calf Extension Machine

Targets the soleus — the deeper, flatter calf muscle underneath the gastrocnemius. Because the soleus crosses only the ankle (not the knee), you have to be seated with the knee bent to target it properly. The seated variation is therefore not a substitute for standing raises — it targets a completely different muscle. Do both for complete calf development.

---

## How to train legs

A complete leg session: one quad movement, one hamstring movement, one calf movement. Two sets each, 1 RIR. If you want more quad volume, add a second quad movement (e.g. Leg Press as the heavy compound, then Leg Extension as isolation).

A solid session: **Hack Squat + Romanian Deadlift + Seated Leg Curl + Standing Calf Raise.** All hard, all progressive, no junk volume.
