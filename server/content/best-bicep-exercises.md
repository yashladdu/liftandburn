## What biceps actually need

The biceps are a relatively small muscle group that gets a lot of indirect work from back training — every row and pulldown loads them. For most people doing adequate back work, you don't need enormous bicep volume on top of that.

Two or three curl variations, 2 working sets each at 1 RIR, is enough to drive excellent bicep growth. What the exercises below have in common: each loads the bicep through a meaningful range of motion and can be taken close to failure safely.

---

## Best Bicep Exercises

### 1. Barbell Curl / EZ-Bar Curl

The foundational bicep mass builder. Barbell or EZ-bar — both work well. The EZ-bar is easier on the wrists and elbows for most people and is the default recommendation unless a straight bar feels fine for you. Heavy, simple, and one of the best movements for building overall bicep size.

Keep your elbows stationary at your sides — don't let them drift forward at the top to swing the weight up. Full range: let the arm fully extend at the bottom before curling back up.

### 2. Dumbbell Curl

Allows each arm to work independently, which is useful for identifying and addressing strength imbalances. The key advantage of dumbbells is **supination** — rotating the wrist from a neutral position to palms-up as you curl up — which produces a stronger bicep contraction than a fixed-grip barbell.

A classic, well-earned reputation.

### 3. Preacher Curl (Machine Preferred)

Excellent for isolating the biceps by removing the ability to cheat. The preacher pad locks your upper arm in place, so the only thing that can move the weight is the bicep. The **machine version** is preferred — constant cable tension throughout the range, easier to push to failure, and easier on the elbow joint.

Targets the lower part of the bicep and builds a great peak when developed.

### 4. Seated Incline Dumbbell Curl

Sit back on an incline bench (around 45-60 degrees) and curl from a fully extended, slightly behind-the-body position. This puts the biceps in a stretched position at the start of the movement, and research consistently shows that training muscles in a lengthened position drives more growth. One of the best bang-for-buck curl variations for this reason.

Let the arm hang fully before each rep — the stretch is the point.

### 5. Bayesian Cable Curl

A cable curl performed with the pulley set low and your arm pulled slightly behind your body at the start, loading the bicep in a stretched position with constant cable tension throughout. Gets the best of both worlds: the stretch load of the incline curl and the constant tension of a cable.

Set up with your back to the cable stack, step forward slightly so your arm is behind your hip, and curl forward and up. One of the most effective curl variations for bicep growth and increasingly popular for good reason.

### 6. Concentration Curl

Elbow braced against the inner thigh, curl with strict isolation. Removes all momentum and forces the bicep to do everything. Good as a finishing movement when you want to feel every rep working. Slightly lower load potential than barbell or cable work, but excellent for mind-muscle connection.

---

## How to train biceps

Two movements is enough for most people. A heavy compound curl (barbell or EZ-bar) plus a stretch-focused variation (incline curl or Bayesian cable curl). Two sets each, 1 RIR, done.

If you want a third movement, add a preacher curl for the isolation stimulus. But don't add volume just to feel like you're doing more — the back training you're already doing is working your biceps too.
