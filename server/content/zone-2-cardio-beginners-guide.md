## What is Zone 2?

Zone 2 is a heart rate range — roughly 60–70% of your maximum heart rate — where your body primarily burns fat for fuel and you can hold a full conversation without gasping.

A simple formula to estimate your max heart rate: **220 minus your age**. Multiply that by 0.6 and 0.7 to get your Zone 2 range.

For a 30-year-old: Zone 2 = 114–133 bpm.

## Why steady-state cardio beats HIIT for most people

High-intensity interval training (HIIT) has dominated fitness content for years. But for hybrid athletes, Zone 2 has several advantages:

- **Less recovery demand** — you can do it the day after a heavy lift without compromising the next session
- **Mitochondrial development** — Zone 2 uniquely stimulates the growth of mitochondria, improving your ability to use oxygen efficiently
- **Fat adaptation** — your body becomes better at burning fat as fuel, preserving glycogen for heavy lifting
- **Sustainable** — you can do 3–5 sessions per week without burning out

## How much Zone 2 should you do?

For beginners starting alongside a lifting programme:

- **Week 1–4:** 2 sessions per week, 20–30 minutes each
- **Week 5–8:** 2–3 sessions, 30–40 minutes each
- **Maintenance:** 3 sessions, 45 minutes each

Total: aim for 90–150 minutes of Zone 2 per week for meaningful cardiovascular adaptations.

## Best Zone 2 activities for lifters

1. **Cycling (indoor or outdoor)** — low impact, easy to control intensity
2. **Brisk walking / incline treadmill** — easiest to stay in zone, zero injury risk
3. **Rowing machine** — full body, great for upper body recovery days
4. **Swimming** — excellent if you have joint issues

Avoid running as your only Zone 2 tool when starting out — impact fatigue makes it hard to stay in the zone consistently.

## How to know you're in Zone 2

The talk test is your best friend. You should be able to speak in full sentences but feel slightly breathless. If you can sing, go harder. If you can't speak, slow down.

A chest strap heart rate monitor is more accurate than wrist-based for this kind of training.

## Common mistakes

- Going too hard and drifting into Zone 3 (junk miles — hard enough to be tiring, not intense enough to be HIIT)
- Doing it on the same day as leg day (your legs won't thank you)
- Skipping it entirely because it "feels too easy" — that's the point
