## What the shoulder actually needs

The shoulder has three heads: the **front delt** (anterior), the **side delt** (lateral), and the **rear delt** (posterior). Most people overtrain the front delt — it gets hammered in every pressing movement — and undertrain the side and rear delts.

The side delt is what creates shoulder width. The rear delt is what creates a 3D, complete shoulder and helps counterbalance all the pressing. If your shoulder training is just pressing, you're only training a third of the muscle.

**2 working sets per exercise, 1 RIR.** That applies here too.

## Best Shoulder Exercises

### 1. Dumbbell Shoulder Press / Machine Shoulder Press

The primary pressing movement for overall shoulder mass. A **machine shoulder press** has real advantages: it's more stable, carries less injury risk, and makes it easier to push close to failure without worrying about dumbbells wobbling near your face. The shoulder joint is vulnerable — a machine path protects it.

**Dumbbells** offer more freedom of movement and a longer range of motion. Either is a great choice. If shoulder health is a concern, the machine is the safer starting point.

### 2. Dumbbell Lateral Raise

The most important exercise for shoulder width. The side delts are what make shoulders look broad from the front and back, and the only way to target them directly is lateral raise variations. Pressing doesn't do it — pressing loads the front delt primarily.

Use a controlled tempo. No swinging, no using momentum to get the weight up. Feel the side delt doing the work on every rep. You'll probably use less weight than you think, and that's correct.

### 3. Cable Lateral Raise

The cable version keeps constant tension on the side delt throughout the range, including at the bottom where dumbbells go slack. Run the cable from the low pulley and raise across your body. This is an excellent alternative to dumbbells and many people find it easier to feel the side delt working.

### 4. Behind-the-Back Cable Lateral Raise

A brilliant variation that changes the resistance curve and loads the side delt in a longer, more stretched position. Set a low cable behind your back and raise to the side — one arm at a time. The angle means the delt is loaded even at the start of the movement, unlike standard lateral raises where the bottom position is essentially unloaded.

Can be done one arm at a time or both simultaneously.

### 5. Cable Y-Raise

A slightly underused movement that targets the side and rear delt simultaneously. From a cable set at around chest height, raise both arms up and out in a Y shape. Excellent for hitting parts of the delt that straight lateral raises and presses don't reach.

### 6. Face Pull

One of the best exercises in any training programme for long-term shoulder health and rear delt development. Set a rope cable at face height, pull to the sides of your face with elbows high, and squeeze the rear delts and external rotators at the top. Keep the weight light — this is about quality of contraction, not load.

Counterbalances the internal rotation bias from all the pressing people do. Do this consistently and your shoulder health will thank you.

### 7. Cable Rear Delt Fly

Direct rear delt isolation. Bend forward at the hips, cables crossed in front of you, and fly both arms out to the sides with a slight bend in the elbow. The cable keeps tension on the rear delt throughout. Small weight, big contraction.

The rear delts are the most neglected part of most people's shoulder training and one of the most important for a balanced, complete look.

---

## How to train shoulders

A complete session: one pressing movement, one lateral raise variation, and one rear delt movement. Two sets each, 1 RIR.

Good pairings: **Machine Shoulder Press + Dumbbell Lateral Raise + Face Pull.** Or rotate the lateral raise to cable or behind-the-back for variety.

Don't skip the rear delt work. Everyone skips it. Everyone has underdeveloped rear delts as a result.
