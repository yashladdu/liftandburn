## The best programme for a beginner

If you're new to training, the best thing you can do is keep it simple. Not because you can't handle complexity — but because complexity isn't what drives results at this stage. Progressive overload on a handful of compound movements, done consistently, is what builds muscle and strength. Everything else is noise.

A 3-day full body programme is the optimal structure for beginners for one reason: **frequency**. When you're new to training, you haven't yet learned how to produce the high levels of effort that make a muscle truly fatigued. Training each muscle group three times per week means more practice, more motor pattern development, and more stimulus than a split programme where each muscle only gets hit once.

As you advance and can train harder, splits become more useful. For now, full body three times a week is the right choice.

---

## The approach

**2 working sets per exercise, 1 rep in reserve.** Every set ends when you have roughly one good rep remaining. Don't go to absolute failure — especially as a beginner, you don't yet have the technique consistency to make grinding out failure reps safe or productive.

**Progressive overload every session.** As a beginner, you should be able to add weight to almost every movement almost every session. This is called beginner gains and it's the most productive training period you'll ever have. Don't waste it by not tracking your weights and adding to them consistently.

**Rest between sessions.** Train Monday, Wednesday, Friday — or any three non-consecutive days. The rest day between each session is when the muscle actually grows.

---

## Weekly schedule

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Full Body A |
| Tuesday | Rest |
| Wednesday | Full Body B |
| Thursday | Rest |
| Friday | Full Body A |
| Saturday | Rest |
| Sunday | Rest |

Alternate between Workout A and Workout B each session. So week 1 is A/B/A, week 2 is B/A/B, and so on. This gives each workout slightly more recovery time between repeats.

---

## Full Body A

**Warm-up:** 5 minutes light cardio (treadmill, bike, or brisk walk). For each exercise, do one warm-up set at around 50% of your working weight before the two working sets.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Squat (barbell, Smith machine, or goblet) | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Bench Press or Chest Press Machine | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2 min |
| Lat Pulldown (neutral grip) | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2 min |
| Romanian Deadlift (dumbbell or barbell) | 2 working sets | 10–12 | 2 min |
| Dumbbell Shoulder Press | 2 working sets | 10–12 | 90 sec |
| Standing Calf Raise | 2 working sets | 15–20 | 60 sec |

## Exercise notes for beginners

**Squat:** If you've never squatted before, start with a goblet squat (dumbbell held at chest) or Smith machine squat before loading a barbell. The pattern matters more than the weight at this stage.

**Romanian Deadlift:** Keep a soft bend in the knees, hinge at the hips, and lower the weight until you feel a stretch in the hamstrings. Don't round the lower back. Start very light — this movement needs to be learned before it's loaded.

**Lat Pulldown:** Drive the elbows down and back — don't think about pulling with your hands. A neutral grip (palms facing each other) is easiest to learn on. Pull to the upper chest.

**Lateral Raise:** Use less weight than you think. Control the movement up and down — no swinging. Feel the side of the shoulder doing the work.

## How to progress

Add weight when you can complete both working sets at the top of the rep range with 1 rep still in reserve. For most beginners on compound movements, that might be every session for the first few months. Track every session — weight used, reps completed.

**When to move on from this programme:** When you're no longer making consistent progress session to session (usually after 3–6 months), it's time to move to a 4-day split like the [upper/lower programme](/articles/4-day-upper-lower-split). By that point you'll have built a solid base of strength and technique.

## Nutrition basics

Training without adequate protein and calories is like building a house without materials. You need:

- **Protein:** 0.7–0.8g per pound of bodyweight (1.6–1.8g per kg) daily. This is the most important number.
- **Calories:** At maintenance or a small surplus (200–300 above) if building muscle is the primary goal. Use the [TDEE calculator](/calculators/tdee) to find your maintenance level.

## What to expect

**Weeks 1–2:** Everything will feel new and awkward. Focus entirely on learning the movement patterns — the weight is almost irrelevant at this stage.

**Weeks 3–8:** This is when beginners typically feel the strongest momentum. Weights go up almost every session. This is the most exciting phase of training — make the most of it.

**Months 3–6:** Progress slows slightly but is still fast compared to experienced lifters. By now the movements feel natural and you're handling real weight. Time to start thinking about the next phase.

The one thing that determines whether this programme works is consistency. Showing up three times a week, adding weight when you can, eating enough protein. That's the entire formula.
