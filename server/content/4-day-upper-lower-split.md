## Why upper/lower works

The upper/lower split is one of the most well-supported training structures in sports science and one of the most practical for real life. You train four days a week, hit every muscle group twice, and keep sessions under an hour. It works for beginners, intermediates, and advanced lifters — the only thing that changes is the load on the bar.

The principle is simple: two days training the upper body (chest, back, shoulders, biceps, triceps), two days training the lower body (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves). Each muscle group gets trained twice per week, which research consistently shows is the sweet spot for hypertrophy — more than once, less than every day.

---

## The approach

Every working set in this programme follows the same rule: **2 working sets, 1 rep in reserve (1 RIR)**. That means each set ends when you have roughly one good rep left in the tank — not when the weight gets moderately uncomfortable, and not to absolute failure. This level of effort drives muscle growth effectively while leaving enough in reserve to recover properly and repeat it next session.

**Progressive overload is the whole game.** When you can complete both working sets at the top of the rep range with 1 RIR, add weight next session. That's it. That's the programme.

## Weekly schedule

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | Upper A |
| Tuesday | Lower A |
| Wednesday | Rest |
| Thursday | Upper B |
| Friday | Lower B |
| Saturday | Rest |
| Sunday | Rest |

Rest days are non-negotiable. Muscle is built during recovery, not during the session itself. If your schedule doesn't suit Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri, any pattern with a rest day between upper and lower works — just avoid training more than two consecutive days.

---

## Upper A — Chest & Back focus

**Warm-up:** 5–10 minutes light cardio or dynamic stretching. For pressing movements, do 1–2 warm-up sets at 50% and 75% of your working weight before the two working sets.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Smith Machine Incline Bench Press | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Lat Pulldown (neutral grip) | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Dumbbell Incline Bench Press | 2 working sets | 10–15 | 2 min |
| Seated Cable Row | 2 working sets | 10–15 | 2 min |
| Pec Deck Fly | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Face Pull | 2 working sets | 15–20 | 90 sec |

**Notes:** Superset opposing muscles where possible — bench press with lat pulldown, incline dumbbell with cable row. Rest only between supersets, not between exercises within a superset. This cuts session time significantly without reducing stimulus.

---

## Lower A — Quad focus

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Hack Squat or Leg Press | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 3 min |
| Romanian Deadlift | 2 working sets | 10–12 | 2–3 min |
| Leg Extension | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Seated Leg Curl | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Standing Calf Raise | 2 working sets | 15–20 | 90 sec |

**Notes:** The hack squat or leg press is your primary quad movement — go heavy and focus on full range of motion. The Romanian deadlift is the primary hamstring movement — control the eccentric, feel the stretch, don't rush it.

---

## Upper B — Shoulders & Arms focus

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Machine Shoulder Press | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| T-Bar Row (chest supported) | 2 working sets | 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Dumbbell Lateral Raise | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| EZ-Bar Curl | 2 working sets | 10–12 | 90 sec |
| Tricep Pushdown (rope) | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Overhead Cable Extension | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Cable Rear Delt Fly | 2 working sets | 15–20 | 90 sec |

**Notes:** Superset biceps and triceps — EZ-bar curl paired with tricep pushdown is a natural combination. Upper B has slightly more arm volume since the chest and back pressing on Upper A already taxes the arms.

---

## Lower B — Posterior chain focus

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Bulgarian Split Squat | 2 working sets | 10–12 each | 2–3 min |
| Deadlift (any variation) | 2 working sets | 6–10 | 3 min |
| Leg Press (feet high) | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 2 min |
| Lying or Seated Leg Curl | 2 working sets | 12–15 | 90 sec |
| Seated Calf Raise | 2 working sets | 15–20 | 90 sec |

**Notes:** Lower B shifts emphasis toward the posterior chain and single-leg work. The Bulgarian split squat is harder than it looks — start lighter than you think and build up. Feet placed higher on the leg press puts more load through the glutes and hamstrings.

## How to progress

**Week to week:** If you completed both working sets at the top of the rep range with 1 RIR, increase the weight by the smallest increment available next session. For dumbbells this is usually 2kg; for machines or barbell, 2.5kg.

**When you can't progress:** If you've stalled on a movement for two or more consecutive sessions, check form first, then check if you're recovering adequately (sleep, protein intake). Stalls are usually a recovery issue before they're a programming issue.

**Minimum effective protein:** Aim for 0.7–0.8g per pound of bodyweight (1.6–1.8g per kg) daily. Use the [macro calculator](/calculators/macros) and [TDEE calculator](/calculators/tdee) to set your daily targets.

## Who this is for

This programme works for anyone who can train four days per week and has access to a commercial gym. It's appropriate for beginners who have learned basic movement patterns, through to intermediate and advanced lifters. The only variable that changes with experience is how much weight is on the bar.
