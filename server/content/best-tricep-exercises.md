## What triceps actually need

The triceps make up roughly two-thirds of the upper arm — developing them matters far more for arm size than bicep work alone. The triceps have three heads: the **long head** (the largest, runs along the back of the upper arm), the **lateral head** (the outer head, visible from the side), and the **medial head** (inner, mostly hidden but important for overall thickness).

Here's the key: **pushdowns only target the lateral and medial heads**. The long head — the biggest part of the tricep — is only fully stretched when the arm is overhead. This is why overhead extension work is non-negotiable if you want complete tricep development. Most people do pushdowns and wonder why their arms aren't growing.

**2 working sets, 1 RIR, progressive overload.** Same as everything else.

## Best Tricep Exercises

### 1. Tricep Pushdown (Bar or Rope)

The most common tricep exercise for good reason. The **rope** allows your hands to spread at the bottom for a stronger contraction and a slightly longer range of motion. The **bar** lets you load heavier. Both are excellent — use the rope when you want to feel the contraction, the bar when you want to load heavier.

Keep your elbows at your sides and drive the weight down and slightly back. Don't let the elbows flare forward or rise up.

### 2. Overhead Tricep Extension (Bar, Rope, or Dumbbell)

Essential for the long head. With your arm overhead, the long head is in a fully stretched position — and as with all muscle groups, training in the stretched position drives more hypertrophy. Do not skip this movement if tricep size is a goal.

Can be performed with a rope cable from above, an EZ-bar, a barbell, or a dumbbell (two hands on one dumbbell). The cable version with a rope is the most comfortable for most people's elbows and allows good range of motion.

### 3. Close-Grip Bench Press

A compound tricep movement that lets you load substantially more weight than isolation exercises. Hands roughly shoulder-width apart (not uncomfortably narrow), elbows tucked close to the body rather than flared out. A great mass builder that also improves pressing strength.

Pairs well in a chest session as a secondary pressing movement that doubles as tricep work.

### 4. Skullcrusher (EZ-Bar or Dumbbell)

A lying tricep extension that targets the long and lateral heads heavily. Lower the weight to your forehead (or slightly behind) with elbows pointing up, then press back up. Use an EZ-bar rather than a straight bar to keep the wrists and elbows comfortable.

Keep the upper arms perpendicular to the floor throughout — don't let the elbows drift back excessively, which shifts the load to the shoulders.

### 5. Dip (Tricep Variation)

Keep your torso **upright** (unlike the chest dip where you lean forward) and elbows tucked close to your sides. This shifts the emphasis from the chest to the triceps. Add weight with a dip belt as you get stronger — this is a movement that rewards progressive loading over time.

One of the best compound tricep movements, often overlooked because people associate dips entirely with the chest.

### 6. Single-Arm Cable Overhead Extension

Cable version of the overhead extension performed one arm at a time. Set the cable at around head height, step forward, and extend the arm overhead. The single-arm version allows a greater range of motion and lets you feel any imbalance between sides. Excellent for targeting the long head with constant cable tension.

## How to train triceps

Cover two categories: **elbow extension at the side** (pushdowns, close-grip bench) and **overhead extension** (skullcrushers, overhead extension). The second category is the one most people miss.

A complete tricep session: **Tricep Pushdown + Overhead Cable Extension**. Or **Close-Grip Bench Press + Rope Pushdown**. Two sets each, 1 RIR.

If you're already doing close-grip bench press in your chest session, your triceps are getting compound work there — your isolation volume can be slightly lower.
